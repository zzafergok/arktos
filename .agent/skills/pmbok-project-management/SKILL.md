# PMBOK Project Management

Tailor project-management practices to the project's uncertainty, delivery model, risk, governance, and organizational context. Do not impose every artifact or process on every project.

## Establish the Project Context

Clarify the intended outcome, sponsor, decision authority, stakeholders, scope boundaries, constraints, delivery approach, regulatory needs, funding, timeline, and definition of success. Distinguish project work with a defined change outcome from ongoing product or operational work.

## Tailor the Approach

Choose predictive, adaptive, or hybrid practices based on requirement stability, feedback speed, technical uncertainty, dependencies, contracting, and governance. Document why the selected controls are proportionate.

Use only the artifacts that improve coordination or decisions, such as:

- charter and success criteria;
- scope and work breakdown;
- milestone or release plan;
- dependency and responsibility map;
- assumptions, decisions, risks, issues, and changes;
- communication and stakeholder-engagement plan;
- quality, procurement, resource, and cost controls;
- benefits and transition plan.

## Plan and Baseline

Decompose outcomes into deliverables and verifiable work. Estimate with ranges and record confidence, dependencies, and assumptions. Establish baselines only where variance must be controlled; avoid pretending uncertain discovery work is deterministic.

## Risk and Change

For each material risk record cause, event, impact, likelihood, exposure, owner, response, trigger, and residual risk. Separate risks from active issues. Evaluate change requests for value, scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, resources, and stakeholder impact before approval by the appropriate owner.

## Monitor Delivery

Report outcomes and forecast, not activity theater. Track the smallest useful set of indicators: milestone confidence, flow or schedule, cost where relevant, quality, risk exposure, decisions, dependencies, and benefit signals. Escalate with options and impact rather than raw status.

## Stakeholders and Communication

Analyze influence, impact, interest, information needs, and desired engagement. Tailor communication cadence and detail. Do not treat the stakeholder matrix as permission to manipulate or conceal information.

## Close and Transition

Confirm acceptance, operational ownership, unresolved risks, contract or financial closure, documentation, lessons, and benefits tracking. A project is not complete merely because delivery tasks are closed.

## Deliverable

Produce the requested plan, register, status report, governance model, or explanation. State the chosen tailoring, assumptions, decision owners, thresholds, and next review point. For certification or edition-specific questions, confirm current PMI terminology and examination guidance before answering.
