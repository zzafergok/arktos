# Marketing Strategy and Product Marketing

Connect verified customer and market insight to positioning, messaging, launch choices, and commercial enablement.

## Establish Context

Clarify product maturity, business model, target geography, sales motion, price, adoption stage, customer evidence, strategic goal, budget, capabilities, and decision horizon. Verify current market, competitor, and channel facts rather than relying on remembered claims.

## Ideal Customer Profile

Define the customer at the level useful for targeting and qualification:

- organization or consumer context;
- triggering situation and urgent problem;
- current alternatives and switching barriers;
- desired outcomes and buying criteria;
- decision participants and adoption risks;
- observable qualification and disqualification signals.

Do not confuse a broad addressable market with an actionable ICP.

## Positioning

Document:

1. competitive alternatives, including doing nothing or internal workarounds;
2. differentiated capabilities that matter to the target customer;
3. value those capabilities create in the customer's language;
4. target segment where that value is most acute;
5. market frame that makes the value easy to understand.

Test positioning against real customer language and sales evidence. Do not manufacture quotes, market size, win rates, or competitor capabilities.

## Messaging

Build a hierarchy from positioning:

- one core promise;
- supporting value pillars;
- proof appropriate to each claim;
- objections and honest responses;
- audience-specific emphasis;
- terminology, tone, and claims boundaries.

Differentiate message strategy from final channel copy. Claims that require legal, regulatory, security, or performance substantiation must be verified before publication.

## Launch and Market Entry

Size the launch by customer impact and business risk rather than internal excitement. Define audience, change story, readiness criteria, channels, enablement, adoption path, measurement, owners, dependencies, rollback or containment, and post-launch learning cadence.

For a new geography or segment, assess demand evidence, localization, regulation, pricing, distribution, support, data handling, competitive response, and operational readiness. Prefer staged tests when uncertainty is high.

## Competitive Intelligence

Track customer-relevant alternatives, not feature grids alone. Record source and date for claims. Separate public fact, customer perception, sales anecdote, and inference. Build battlecards around discovery questions, differentiation, traps to avoid, proof, and objection handling without misrepresentation.

## Measurement

Choose metrics that reflect the requested outcome: qualified demand, conversion, win rate, sales velocity, activation, adoption, retention, expansion, awareness, or message comprehension. Define baseline, segment, timeframe, source, owner, and guardrails.

## Deliverable

Lead with the recommended target, positioning, and go-to-market choices. Include evidence, assumptions, message hierarchy, launch or enablement plan, metrics, risks, non-goals, and unresolved research needs.
