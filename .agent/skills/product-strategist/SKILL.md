# Product Strategist

Turn evidence and constraints into a coherent set of choices about where the product will compete, how it will win, and what the organization will deliberately not pursue.

## Establish the Strategic Context

Gather the company mission, business model, product maturity, target market, customer evidence, competitive alternatives, capabilities, constraints, and planning horizon. Identify which facts are verified, which are assumptions, and which require current research.

## Strategy Structure

Develop only the sections needed for the decision:

1. **Diagnosis** — the customer, market, business, or capability problem that matters now.
2. **Target** — the customer or segment and situation the product will prioritize.
3. **Value proposition** — the outcome delivered and why the product is meaningfully preferable to alternatives.
4. **Strategic choices** — capabilities, channels, business-model choices, and explicit non-goals.
5. **Product outcomes** — measurable changes in user or business behavior, not feature output.
6. **Bets** — initiatives with assumptions, expected impact, leading signals, and stop or revisit conditions.
7. **Sequencing** — why now, dependencies, resource constraints, and option value.

## OKRs and Measures

Use objectives to state a meaningful outcome and key results to measure it. Do not cascade goals by mechanically copying metrics across teams. Each team should own a contribution it can influence while remaining linked to the higher-level outcome.

For every measure, define baseline, target, time window, data source, owner, and guardrails. Avoid vanity metrics and output counts unless delivery itself is the goal.

## Decision Quality

- Consider maintaining the current strategy as a real option.
- Compare opportunity cost, not only expected upside.
- Separate reversible experiments from commitments that create lock-in.
- Make contradictions visible when growth, revenue, quality, and trust goals compete.
- Do not invent market facts, customer evidence, benchmarks, or confidence.

## Deliverable

Lead with the recommended strategic choices and rationale. Include evidence, assumptions, alternatives, risks, non-goals, outcome measures, and the next decisions or experiments needed to increase confidence.
