# Product Manager Toolkit

Choose the smallest product-management artifact or method that improves the current decision. Do not produce a full ceremony set by default.

## Frame the Work

Identify the decision, decision owner, target user, current evidence, deadline, constraints, and what would change the decision. Keep problem discovery separate from solution commitment.

## Discovery

- Express the problem as an observed behavior, unmet need, or outcome gap.
- Record evidence with its source and recency.
- Distinguish facts, interpretations, assumptions, and hypotheses.
- Use interviews to understand motivations and context, not to ask users to design features.
- Combine qualitative evidence with product data when available.

## Prioritization

Choose criteria appropriate to the decision. RICE, impact-effort, cost of delay, or weighted scoring can organize judgment but do not replace it. Define scales before scoring, expose uncertainty, and run sensitivity checks when close scores determine the outcome.

For RICE, document reach, impact scale, confidence basis, effort unit, and calculation. Never fabricate values to complete a table.

## Roadmaps

Prefer outcome- or problem-oriented roadmap items. For each item, capture rationale, intended outcome, evidence, owner, dependencies, confidence, guardrails, and validation signal. Use dates only where there is a real commitment or dependency.

## Experiments

Define the hypothesis, target population, primary metric, guardrails, minimum meaningful effect, duration or stopping rule, and decision rule before launching. Consider qualitative tests or prototypes when they can reduce uncertainty faster than production changes.

## Product Requirements

Include the problem, users, goals, non-goals, requirements, states and edge cases, dependencies, analytics, rollout, risks, and acceptance criteria. Match detail to the decision stage; do not disguise unresolved product choices as engineering requirements.

## Communication

Tailor the readout to the audience while preserving the same underlying facts. Lead with the decision, evidence, implications, and requested action. Make tradeoffs and uncertainty explicit.

## Completion

Deliver the requested artifact with enough source context to audit its reasoning. State missing evidence and the next lowest-cost step to improve confidence.
