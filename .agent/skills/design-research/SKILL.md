# Mobile Design Research

Use evidence to identify and prioritize mobile product opportunities, accounting for platform context, acquisition, activation, retention, and recurring usage.

## Frame the Decision

Define the product decision, target users, lifecycle stage, geography, platform, business model, current evidence, constraints, and what new evidence could change the decision.

## Evidence Sources

Use the smallest appropriate combination:

- interviews or contextual inquiry for motivations and real-world use;
- usability tests for interaction problems;
- analytics for observed funnels, cohorts, and retention;
- support and feedback for recurring pain;
- App Store or Play reviews for directional public feedback;
- competitive products for alternatives and market expectations.

Store reviews and competitor observations are biased samples. Do not treat frequency in public comments as population prevalence without supporting data.

## Synthesis

Separate observation, interpretation, assumption, and recommendation. Cluster evidence into jobs, triggers, barriers, unmet needs, and desired outcomes. Retain contradictory cases and segment differences.

Describe each opportunity with:

- affected user and situation;
- evidence and source quality;
- current behavior or workaround;
- user and business consequence;
- confidence and missing evidence;
- potential outcome, not a predetermined feature.

## Prioritization

Compare user harm or value, frequency, strategic relevance, retention or revenue connection, reach, confidence, effort, platform constraints, and reversibility. Identify the smallest experiment that could reduce the most important uncertainty.

## Deliverable

Lead with prioritized opportunities and supporting evidence. Include segments, journey or lifecycle context, limitations, design principles, experiment ideas, and next research questions. Do not fabricate participants, quotes, analytics, store data, or competitor facts.
